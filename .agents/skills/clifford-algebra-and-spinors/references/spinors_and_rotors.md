# Spinors, Rotors & The Spin Group $\text{Spin}(p, q)$

This reference details the construction, algebraic properties, and geometric transformations of Spinors, Rotors, and the Spin Group in $n$-dimensional Clifford Algebras.

---

## 1. The Even Subalgebra $\mathcal{Cl}^+(p, q)$ & Spin Group

The grade involution $\hat{A}$ decomposes $\mathcal{Cl}(p, q)$ into even and odd grading subspaces:

$$\mathcal{Cl}(p, q) = \mathcal{Cl}^+(p, q) \oplus \mathcal{Cl}^-(p, q)$$

The **even subalgebra** $\mathcal{Cl}^+(p, q) = \bigoplus_{k \text{ even}} \mathcal{Cl}^k(p, q)$ forms a closed sub-algebra of dimension $2^{n-1}$.

### The Spin and Pin Groups
- **$\text{Pin}(p, q)$**: The group of normalized versors generated by products of unit vectors under geometric multiplication:
  $$\text{Pin}(p, q) = \{ V = v_1 v_2 \dots v_k \in \mathcal{Cl}(p, q) \mid v_i^2 = \pm 1, \; V \tilde{V} = \pm 1 \}$$
- **$\text{Spin}(p, q)$**: The intersection of $\text{Pin}(p, q)$ with the even subalgebra $\mathcal{Cl}^+(p, q)$:
  $$\text{Spin}(p, q) = \{ R \in \mathcal{Cl}^+(p, q) \mid R \tilde{R} = 1 \}$$
- **Double Covering**: $\text{Spin}(p, q)$ forms a double cover of the special orthogonal group $\text{SO}^+(p, q)$:
  $$1 \to \mathbb{Z}_2 \to \text{Spin}(p, q) \xrightarrow{\rho} \text{SO}^+(p, q) \to 1$$
  where $\rho(R)(v) = R v \tilde{R}$. Both $+R$ and $-R$ produce the identical spatial rotation.

## 2. Rotors in $n$ Dimensions

A **Rotor** $R$ is an even multivector satisfying the normalization condition:

$$R \tilde{R} = 1$$

### Exponential Representation
Any simple rotation through angle $\theta$ in the plane defined by a unit bivector $B$ ($B^2 = -1$) is generated by the exponential map:

$$R = \exp\left(-\frac{\theta}{2} B\right) = \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) - B \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)$$

### Vector Transformation (Sandwich Product)
To rotate an arbitrary vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$\mathbf{v}' = R \mathbf{v} \tilde{R} = R \mathbf{v} R^{-1}$$

- **Parallel component** ($\mathbf{v}_\parallel \wedge B = 0$): $\mathbf{v}_\parallel' = \cos(\theta)\mathbf{v}_\parallel - \sin(\theta)(\mathbf{v}_\parallel \cdot B)$
- **Orthogonal component** ($\mathbf{v}_\perp \cdot B = 0$): $\mathbf{v}_\perp' = \mathbf{v}_\perp$ (invariant under rotation)

## 3. Lie Algebra Connection: $\mathfrak{so}(p, q) \cong \mathcal{Cl}^2(p, q)$

The space of bivectors $\mathcal{Cl}^2(p, q)$ with the commutator bracket $[A, B] = \frac{1}{2}(A B - B A)$ is directly isomorphic to the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(p, q)$:

| Lie Algebra Concept | Clifford Algebra Equivalent |
| :--- | :--- |
| Lie Algebra element | Bivector $B \in \mathcal{Cl}^2(p, q)$ |
| Lie Bracket $[X, Y]$ | Commutator $X \times Y = \frac{1}{2}(X Y - Y X)$ |
| Exponential Map $\exp(X)$ | Multivector Taylor series $\exp(B) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{B^k}{k!}$ |
| Infinitesimal generator | Derivative $\dot{R} = -\frac{1}{2} \Omega R$ ($\Omega$ = angular velocity bivector) |

## 4. Spinors as Elements of Minimal Left Ideals

In physics and advanced geometry, a **spinor** $\psi$ is an element of a minimal left ideal $\mathcal{I} = \mathcal{Cl}(p, q) f$, generated by a primitive idempotent $f$ ($f^2 = f$):

### A. Pauli Spinors in $\mathcal{Cl}(3, 0)$
In 3D Euclidean space $\mathcal{Cl}(3, 0)$, using $f = \frac{1}{2}(1 + e_3)$:
$$\psi = (\alpha_0 + \alpha_1 e_1 e_2 + \alpha_2 e_2 e_3 + \alpha_3 e_3 e_1) f \iff \begin{pmatrix} z_1 \\ z_2 \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{C}^2$$
Pauli matrices correspond directly to spatial basis vectors:
$$\sigma_1 \leftrightarrow e_1, \quad \sigma_2 \leftrightarrow e_2, \quad \sigma_3 \leftrightarrow e_3, \quad i \leftrightarrow I_3 = e_1 e_2 e_3$$

### B. Dirac Spinors in Spacetime Algebra $\mathcal{Cl}(1, 3)$
In 4D Minkowski space $\mathcal{Cl}(1, 3)$ (signature $+ - - -$):
- Dirac $\gamma$-matrices satisfy $\gamma^\mu \gamma^\nu + \gamma^\nu \gamma^\mu = 2 \eta^{\mu\nu}$.
- The Dirac equation is formulated coordinate-free:
  $$\nabla \psi I_4 \sigma_3 = m \psi \gamma_0$$
- Spinors $\psi \in \mathcal{Cl}^+(1, 3)$ contain 8 real components, unifying the Lorentz boost and spatial rotation into a single spacetime rotor.

## 5. Multi-Rotor Decomposition in $n\text{D}$

In higher dimensions ($n \ge 4$), a general rotation cannot always be represented by a single planar bivector because bivectors can have rank $> 2$.

By the **Euler-Cartan Theorem**, every element $R \in \text{Spin}(n)$ decomposes into the commuting product of at most $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ simple orthogonal planar rotations:

$$R = \prod_{k=1}^{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor} \exp\left(-\frac{\theta_k}{2} B_k\right), \quad B_j \wedge B_k = 0 \; (j \neq k)$$

- **In 4D ($\text{Spin}(4) \cong \text{Spin}(3) \times \text{Spin}(3)$)**: Any 4D rotation is a double rotation (isoclinic if $\theta_1 = \pm \theta_2$).
- **In 5D / CGA ($\text{Spin}(4, 1)$)**: Unifies 3D Euclidean motors, dilations, and spherical inversions into a single 16-component rotor.
